Flame Path · working title

A longer stay with the practice.

An immersion for people who want to understand shamanic yoga well enough to practise it deeply, and perhaps to guide others. Not a claim of an outside certificate unless Martina adds one.

Who it is for

Come if several of these are true

  • You already have a practice, even a shy one, and you want it to go further than a weekly class.
  • You are curious about holding space for other people, without pretending you are finished.
  • You want movement, breath, and a shamanic way of listening taught as one thing.
  • You can give a string of weekends across [number of weekends], not a single intensive.
  • You are willing to be a student in public, including when you teach a short piece to the group.
  • You live near Barcelona, or you can travel in for the in-person meetings.

A clear no is also useful

This path is a poor fit if you want a fast credential, a large lecture, or a promise that a certificate will employ you. If a recognising body is involved, it will be named here as [accreditation, if any]. Until then, assume none.

What you study

Themes, not a borrowed syllabus

Movement that tells the truth

Alignment as usefulness: joints happy, breath available, ego not driving the shape.

Breath you can share

Simple patterns, and the difference between an invitation and a push.

Energy without theatre

How to speak about what you sense without inventing a diagnosis or a spectacle.

Adapting the room

Different bodies, different days, and language that does not shame either.

Ritual in proportion

Small acts — a candle, a pause, a question — and the line where ritual becomes performance.

Practice teaching

Short turns in front of the group, with feedback that is specific and kind.

What you leave with

A practice you can stand inside

  • A personal sequence you can repeat without a teacher in the room.
  • Language for breath, attention, and limit.
  • Experience of guiding a short piece, if you want that path.
  • Notes from the group, yours to keep.
  • A clearer sense of whether teaching is your work, or practice is enough.

Any certificate, letter, or hour-count is [accreditation, if any].
